# Road allowances, area scope and acceptance

## Roads
Use the width printed on the source when present. Ordinary Alberta road allowances may be 20.12 m where applicable; correction-line roads, registered road plans and contrary evidence require their own legal/control interpretation. Width is a perpendicular strip dimension, not permission to change a surveyed course. Construct supported road limits; trim acquisition only after separately locating the controlling R/W and its opposite boundary. Preserve full pin-to-pin observations in the control graph. Exclude the road strip from acquisition area unless the source explicitly says otherwise.

## Width and areas
Locate the controlling subject side from source observations, then derive its companion side by stated perpendicular width with analytical miters/intersections. Check width independently; never move accepted control to satisfy it. Preserve each printed area with its legal quarter/parcel scope. A combined area check is valid only if the printed portions are demonstrably non-overlapping parts of the same area. An absent area calculation is NOT_EVALUATED, not PASS. An area mismatch is REVIEW_REQUIRED and must not be repaired by arbitrary shape edits.

## Verification and states
Verify source-course completeness, bearings/distances, direction, CRS/ground-grid, control residuals, section evidence, road terminals, width, closure where applicable, area, lineweight/topology and source overlay. A survey control graph may legitimately contain open chains; do not invent a closure. The acquisition polygon must close when its legal boundaries are established.
- PASS: complete source-supported graph, accepted geometry, independent checks and reopened CAD audit.
- REVIEW_REQUIRED: source ambiguity, unresolved endpoint/control, missing independent check, or material discrepancy.
- FAIL: unsupported boundary, omitted required dimensioned course, fabricated control or false acceptance.
- BLOCKED_MISSING_TOOL: deterministic construction/readback unavailable.
A valid DXF file is not proof of a valid reconstruction.
