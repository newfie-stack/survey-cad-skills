## Inspected clauses supporting bounded checks

The following are concise paraphrases of the retrieved content manual, with
PDF page numbers matching printed page numbers. They identify possible
mechanical observations; they do not certify the full clause.

| Clause | Locator and inspected requirement | Evidence and limit |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1.1, 1.1.3–1.1.4 | Page 6: general legibility/scale, sheet-size limits and an enumerated set of main-plan scales with exceptions. | PDF dimensions or a selected printed ratio can be measured; original plotting size, legibility, actual plotted scale and exceptions need separate evidence. Insets are not the main-plan ratio. |
| 1.2.1.1–1.2.1.5 | Pages 6–7: plan-type heading, purpose/activity wording, legal description, agreement with the application form and municipality. | Requires source-located fields and the relevant application/table entry. A phrase found somewhere in the PDF does not establish agreement or correct subject selection. |
| 1.2.2.1, 1.2.2.3 | Page 7: legal applicant name agrees with client registry and form; both graphical and numerical scales shown. | Registry/form comparisons require those records. Native numerical scale extraction does not inspect the graphical bar. |
| 1.3.3.5 | Page 7: activities in unsurveyed territory have that status indicated in the plan body. | A selected phrase check is a bounded observation; the clause does not prescribe one exact phrase. Applicability needs source evidence, and an annotation does not establish Crown ownership. |
| 1.3.6.1–1.3.6.6 | Page 8: area notation and hectares, total activity area, conditional within/outside and FMA breakdowns, changing widths, and specified linear-activity lengths. | Text/table arithmetic is distinct from boundary area and exclusion reconciliation. Part 4 exceptions, spatial overlap, ownership and disposition status need their own evidence. |
| 1.3.7.1, 1.3.7.3 | Page 9: details have unique identities matching main-plan descriptions, and existing plans are plotted and labelled consistently with the main body. | Selected same-subject text comparisons cover label agreement only; unique identities across all details, meaning, plotted positions and extents require further evidence. |
| 3.5.2.1 | Page 13: hybrid coordinate-table point numbers start at 1 and increment without omissions or inserted variants. | An executed sequence check covers an identified complete supplied table. It does not establish the plan's point-ordering or cadastral correctness. |
| 3.5.2.2 | Page 13: northings/eastings use two decimal places without spaces or commas. | Inspect original printed strings; normalized numbers cannot prove printed formatting. Missing or untrusted table transcription remains unresolved. |
| 3.5.2.3 | Page 13: coordinate types are EC, XC, RC and OC. | Type-code validation does not accept the actual legal/evidence role assigned to a point. |
| 3.5.6.2 | Page 14: at least two OCs shown on the plan. | Counting supplied table entries is a partial observation. Whether the corresponding points are shown in the plan body requires linked or visual evidence. |
| 3.5.6.3 | Page 14: every EC/XC/RC is within 10 km of an OC. | Needs complete relevant points in the correct metric reference system, accepted point roles and evidence binding. A distance diagnostic does not accept control, survey accuracy, coordinate provenance or reconstruction. |

Public Lands Survey Manual v2.3 section 4.3, PDF pages 10–11, describes the
hybrid point roles and requires the hybrid reference system UTM NAD83 (CSRS)
Epoch 2002; ATS v4.1 coordinates cannot be used to geo-reference the hybrid
survey. ALSA MSP Part C section 7, PDF pages 48–49 / printed pages 42–43,
contains additional evidence and accuracy obligations. The coordinate-table
and distance observations above do not execute those obligations.

## CAD, GIS and service distinctions

- Geo-Referencing Guide v4.0 section 4.1, page 6, addresses CAD files: the RP
  identification is on layer 35; its symbol centre must match the printed grid
  coordinate; the file is oriented to grid with the orientation source in
  metadata. A working-DWG `EVIDENCE` layer and a submission layer `35` remain
  distinct conventions. A layer-name match alone does not verify the RP symbol,
  coordinate match, survey control or entire CAD package.
- PCS guide pages 5–7 distinguishes CAD layers, CAD packages and shapefiles.
  CAD boundary layer 28, RP layer 35 and activity-code layer 40 have different
  content requirements; ATS layer 17 is optional in the listed table. Preserve
  its explicit AEP-system scope. Manual 018 section 3.1, PDF pages 8–10,
  references the guide but also has AER-specific application/CAD exceptions.
- The [current OneStop help page](https://www.aer.ca/applications-and-notices/onestop/onestop-help)
  links the [August 2025 FAQ](https://www.aer.ca/documents/onestop/FAQ_OneStop-PLA-Shapefile.pdf)
  and [June 2020 spatial QRG](https://www.aer.ca/documents/onestop/QRG_ImportSpatialDataPLA.pdf).
  FAQ Q2, page 1, states there is no OneStop-shapefile georeferencing requirement
  and recommends DIDs/base-feature alignment for optimal results. Q15, page 4,
  preserves PDF plan requirements. Q21, page 5, distinguishes standalone or
  unassociated RTF CAD exceptions. Do not turn a recommendation into a mandate
  or a shapefile statement into a PDF/CAD waiver.
- The QRG page 4 contains older formal-boundary coincidence and projection
  wording. FAQ Q8, page 3, repeats one projection name twice in the retrieved
  source; do not invent a corrected second name. Artifact-specific template,
  application validation and current instructions are needed before normative
  CRS or alignment findings. Diagnostic comparisons may report the actual CRS
  and differences while leaving those normative questions unresolved.
- The [PCS page](https://www.alberta.ca/plan-confirmation-service) still requires
  Alberta.ca Account access effective December 5, 2025. That replaces the guide's
  older account steps; it does not itself replace layer standards.

ALSA MSP Part C 4.8 is PDF page 39 / printed page 33. It distinguishes surveyor
sketches, requires a stated derivation method and ALS declaration, and describes
the additional explanatory boundary note as a recommendation. The task-force
notice's shorthand reference to 4.8.3 does not create a separate adopted rule.
